# Helion Architecture: Water Optics

Part of the [Helion architecture](../ARCHITECTURE.md). Realistic water (`reflections.realisticWater`) after the SEUS Renewed comparison of 2026-10-25: the owner asked for open water, reflections and the underwater view to match SEUS Renewed and go beyond it. A first version with strong capillary waves and milky water looked stylized to the owner ("like Team Fortress 2 or Valorant"); they want clear water with detailed reflections, so the waves below are gentle and the water clear. SEUS Renewed was studied from its archive and screenshots for ideas only (rule T1); every technique below is Helion's own code with its own parameters.

## Capillary Waves and Filtered Roughness

1. `helionCapillarySlope` (`helion_water_waves.glsl`) adds four octaves of analytic gradient noise slopes on top of the swell: wavelengths of about 0.87, 0.48, 0.27 and 0.16 blocks, slope amplitudes 0.022 to 0.012, each turned, stretched across the wind (0.5 to 0.9) and drifting fast (0.4 to 1 noise cells per second). A large gust noise (about 22 blocks, drifting) scales them from 0.6 to 1.15, so calm and rippled patches alternate, and rain raises them by up to 60 %. They only wobble the reflections and split the sun into small glints; at 0.075 they broke the sky reflection into hard white patches that looked painted.
2. Each octave fades out once its wavelength covers fewer than about three pixels (the same footprint test as the swell), and the slope variance it loses (`helionCapillaryLostVariance`) becomes GGX roughness, `sqrt(2 variance)`. Distant water therefore turns into a glittering path instead of aliased sparkles or a flat mirror. The surface pass writes the roughness into the alpha of `helion:water_tint` (0.5 to 1 for 0 to 0.5 roughness, 0.25 marks falling water, 0 no water; `helion_water.glsl`).
3. The swell octaves are plain gradient noise again (the crested ridges are gone, they drew cartoon-like lines), and their slope is analytic (`helionNoiseSlope` through the turn, shear and stretch of each octave) instead of four height samples, which cut the hashes per water pixel from about 96 to 24. The far calm of the swell is weaker (60 % instead of 35 % beyond 80 blocks), because the filtered roughness now keeps distant water from sparkling.
4. `helion_noise_slope.glsl` holds the analytic gradient noise and its slope, shared by the capillaries, the caustics and the underwater light shafts.

## Reflections

1. Fresnel is Schlick with F0 0.02 (water, IOR 1.33), lowered at grazing angles by the roughness (`max(1 - roughness, F0)` as the grazing value), so rough distant water does not turn into a white mirror.
2. Sun and moon glints are a GGX specular term (Smith visibility, Fresnel on the half vector) with the combined roughness of a base 0.012 (about the size of the sun disc) and the filtered roughness, times an irradiance of 4 for the sun in the hue of the sky near it and 0.03 for the moon, capped at 16 before tone mapping. Close capillaries make sharp sparkles, distant water a broad glitter path, and the glint still vanishes where the reflected ray hit geometry.
3. Realistic water always runs the screen space reflection march for water pixels, also with the screen space reflections setting off (that setting still decides polished blocks), so trees, shores and mobs mirror in the water instead of only the sky. Measured cost: see below.
4. Screen space reflections start their march with a per pixel jitter of 0.5 to 1.5 times the first step, which temporal anti-aliasing turns into smooth reflections instead of steps. The jitter moves with real time (`FootprintCount.z`) and only runs while temporal anti-aliasing is on (`FootprintCount.w`): the first version took the time from the rain wetness, which stands still in dry weather, so the pattern froze into dotted edges on distant reflections.

## Clouds Beyond the Screen

`CloudStage` renders a cloud sky map whenever a water reflection setting is on (`cloud_sky_map`, 192 x 192 RGBA16_FLOAT, `clouds/march.fsh` with `HELION_CLOUD_SKY_MAP`): the upper hemisphere in an octahedral layout (`helion_cloud_sky_map.glsl`), marched with 60 % of the primary steps of the cloud quality, every frame. The water composite uses the on-screen cloud target where the reflected direction is on screen (faded over the outer 8 %) and the sky map elsewhere, so reflected clouds no longer stop at the screen edge. This works on every card; ray tracing does not help here, because the clouds are a volume, not geometry in the scene.

## Water Color and Clarity

`helion_water_optics.glsl` derives absorption and scattering per channel from the biome tint, the depth of the water column and the turbidity; the surface composite and the underwater pass both use it.

1. Pure water absorbs 0.36, 0.058 and 0.05 per block (red, green, blue; blue close to green, so deep and underwater views turn teal rather than navy). Dissolved organic matter adds (0.45, 0.42, 2.0) times its amount, suspended particles scatter (0.82, 1, 1.08) times theirs, slightly tinted toward the biome color.
2. The biome is read from the tint: swamp water (green above blue) gets more particles (0.25) and much organic matter (1.0 of an absorption shape 0.45, 0.42, 2.0 per block), so even one block of it is dark, murky olive green from above like under water; turquoise tints (warm and lukewarm oceans: green close to blue) are up to 75 % clearer; ordinary water has 0.025 particles in shallow water, falling to 0.015 between 4 and 16 blocks of depth, so lakes and rivers are clear turquoise over their bottom and deep ocean is dark blue.
3. Rain adds 0.09 particles (the published rain level).
4. From above, the bottom is attenuated along the view ray plus the depth of the column (light coming down), and the water adds `albedo * light * 0.4 * (1 - exp(-extinction * thickness))`, where the light is the sky ambient (35 % of its hue, the rest its luminance), 0.45 of the sun in its sky hue and forward sun scattering, so the color of the water comes from its own albedo and not from the blue sky. . Caustics on the bottom seen from above use the same `helion_caustics.glsl`, but only on bottom surfaces that face up: the facing comes from the bottom points of the neighbor pixels, so walls and steps under water get no vertical caustic streaks.
5. Caustics on the bottom seen from above use the same `helion_caustics.glsl`, but only on bottom surfaces that face up: the facing comes from the bottom points of the neighbor pixels, so walls and steps under water get no vertical caustic streaks.

## Under Water

`UnderwaterStage` copies the scene and redraws it (`water/underwater.fsh`, no blending), so the extinction is per channel.

1. `VanillaWeatherSource` reports an `UnderwaterView`: the camera sky light, the height of the water surface above the eye and of the floor below it (block scans of up to 48 blocks) and the biome water color at the camera. The vanilla underwater fog is pushed out to at least 384 blocks; Helion owns the underwater look.
2. Light shafts: 16 jittered steps along the view ray (up to 32 blocks). Each step takes the sun or moon light refracted into the water, attenuated by the water above the step along that direction, and a shaft pattern: one octave of ridged gradient noise (4 blocks across, sharpness 6) of the point where the refracted light ray entered the surface, wider and softer with depth, so beams of focused light slant down from the surface and drift with the waves. A Henyey-Greenstein phase (g 0.65) makes them bright toward the sun. The beams start where the light enters the surface and keep their pattern only near it: the pattern blends into its mean glow with depth below the surface (e^(-depth / 8)), with distance from the camera (e^(-distance / 10)) and when looking along the light (dot above 0.75 to 0.97). Without this the far, sparsely sampled parts of the rays formed fans that radiated from one point in front of the player. Ambient sky light adds isotropic scattering, and the rest of the ray beyond 32 blocks is integrated in closed form.
3. Surfaces keep their lighting but their color shifts with the water above them (absorption normalized to the same luminance), and surfaces facing the refracted sun get caustics from `helion_caustics.glsl` at the depth of the point below the surface: three octaves of moving noise refracted and focused at 0.55 times the depth, from 0.2 to 6 times the light (octave heights 0.08, 0.02, 0.004). `helionCausticGain` mostly brightens: below the mean the light only drops by a quarter of the difference, so the gaps between caustic lines no longer look like dirt stains on the blocks (from above and under water).
4. Everything is attenuated by `exp(-extinction * distance)` up to the surface; the surface seen from below glows with the water's scattering color and shows the bright Snell window.

## Clouds

The clear weather cloud coverage is 0.46 (was 0.55), so open skies show more blue between the clouds; rain still raises it toward 0.85.

## Night

The eye barely sees into water at night, so the water body seen from above (bottom and scattering) keeps 45 % of its light without daylight and the glow of the surface seen from below 20 %; under water the moon lights the shafts with 0.025 to 0.05 per channel. Lamps still reflect through the screen space and traced reflections.

## Shore Waves (2026-10-25)

The owner asked for waves that roll in from the open water and break on beaches.

1. `WaterShoreDistance` turns the shore mask (land columns of the 128 x 128 block window) into a distance to the nearest land every frame: a two pass 3-4 chamfer transform in window space up to 16 blocks, uploaded as an R8 texture with the world mapping of the mask.
2. `helionShoreWave` samples it (bilinear, repeat) with the gradient of the distance as the seaward direction. Within 15 blocks of land a wave train travels toward the shore at 1.7 radians per second: its wave number grows from 0.85 to 2.04 per block toward the shore (shoaling, integrated so crests stay continuous), the phase is warped by a 17 block noise so crests bend, and the crest shape is `(0.5 + 0.5 sin)^2.5`, so crests are sharp and troughs long. The height (0.14) rises from 15 to 3 blocks off the shore and fades in the last 0.8 blocks; a fetch test 6 blocks seaward (open water between 3 and 9 blocks) keeps ponds and narrow channels calm, and the waves keep 30 % over water deeper than 9 blocks (column depth from the surface pass thickness), so walls into deep water get no big rollers.
3. Breaking foam: on crests above 0.6 of their height within 5 blocks of land, broken up by noise and limited to water shallower than 2 to 6 blocks, the composite mixes in up to 90 % white lit by the water light. It follows the crests toward the shore and dissolves, unlike the removed foam that sat on every wall. The composite gets the game time (seconds modulo 1200, the same clock as `GameTime` in the surface pass) in `WaterEnvironment.y`.
4. Both fade out from 44 to 60 blocks with the shore window. Visual test `water_ocean_shore` (a stepped sand beach at the east end of the ocean basin).

## Translucent Entities in Reflections (2026-10-25)

In third person the player did not mirror in the water: player models (and slimes) use `entityTranslucent`, which `TransparentGeometryStage` drew after the screen space reflection pass. `TranslucentFeatureStage` now draws the translucent entity features right after the solid features while a water reflection setting is on, so the surface pass, the refraction copy and the reflections include them; vanilla also draws them before translucent terrain, so nothing visible changes order. Visual test: a frozen slime floats on the lake in `water_lake`.

## Glass (2026-10-25)

The surface pass once held one translucent layer: the nearest one. Glass in front of water hid the water, so the realistic water composite never ran behind a window, and aerial fog, rain and light shafts stopped at the glass (they use the translucent depth). The pass now draws the translucent layer twice with the same pipelines: `helion:water_surface` keeps only water sprites, `helion:glass_surface` and `helion:glass_tint` keep everything else (ice, glass, stained glass, panes) with its face normal, the glossy marker `-1 - thickness` (`helionEncodeGlossy`, so the distance to the opaque scene behind survives) and the texel color and alpha. The glass pass has its own uniform ring, because one ring slot written twice in a frame keeps only the second write.

1. The composite draws water first, then glass on top when the glass is nearer. Without realistic water glass is the old faint Fresnel reflection over vanilla translucency.
2. With realistic water the composite replaces glass pixels: the refraction copy behind the glass, wobbled by up to 0.25 % of the screen by a world space noise (only in the clear part of the texture and only where no water lies behind, whose own refraction already bent it), the aerial fog of the physical sky up to the surface behind (skipped when the thickness reached 64 blocks, so the sky is not fogged twice), then multiplied by the stain: the linear texel color times 1.2, weighted by the texel alpha times 1.6 (frame texels with alpha 0.9 or more keep their own look). Black stained glass has a texel alpha of only about 0.3, so without the gain it barely darkened.
3. Reflection by Schlick with F0 0.04 (IOR 1.5). Where no water lies behind the glass, screen space reflections and ray traced reflections trace the glass pixel (both read the glass target where the water target is empty) and the composite uses them like for water; over water they trace the water, and the glass reflects the sky only.

Clouds behind glass (2026-10-26): the cloud composite of `TransparentGeometryStage` only draws where the translucent depth is sky, so glass in front of the sky hid the clouds. Where glass has nothing but sky behind it (thickness 64 blocks), the glass composite now blends the resolved on-screen cloud target into the sky it sees, with the same saturation (1.35) and Neutral encoding as the cloud composite.

Water against glass (2026-10-26): vanilla draws a water face that touches glass or leaves with the `water_overlay` sprite. The surface pass only knew the still and flowing sprites, so those faces landed in the glass target and showed as blue framed glass around waterfalls and aquariums; the surface uniform now also carries the overlay bounds (`WaterOverlay`, appended at the end of `HelionWaterSurface`).

Glass, second pass (2026-10-27, owner request: smooth but visibly glass, rain drops): the body of a pane takes one smooth tone from the hue of its texel (color divided by its brightest channel), from 6 % for clear glass to 80 % for saturated stained glass, and dark glass shades to 55 %, so the streaks and spots of the glass texture no longer show; frame texels keep 30 % of their own color; the reflection never drops below 5 %. In rain, glass with sky light above 0.85 carries drops: beads on a 7 per block grid (up to 55 % of the cells, radius 0.14 to 0.32 of a cell) and, on upright panes, running drops in 2.5 columns per block that slide down at 0.35 to 0.9 blocks per second with a fading trail; drops bend the view behind them by up to 1.2 % of the screen, darken it by 12 % and catch a highlight of the reflection. Visual test: `glass_window_rain`.

Falling water (2026-10-26): falling faces used the thickness to the opaque scene behind, which was up to 64 blocks where sky or the far terrain lay behind a waterfall, so waterfalls showed as solid teal blocks; their thickness is now at most half a block, a thin sheet. The falling foam was a product of two sines, which drew framed, bracket shaped lines; it is now two octaves of value noise stretched along the fall (4 cells per block across, 0.6 down) moving down at 2.2 cells per second, at 55 % strength. Visual test: `waterfall_close`, `waterfall_above`.

Visual test: `glass_window` and `glass_window_rain` look north over the lake through a black stained glass pane, a plain glass pane and a light blue stained glass block; `tinted_glass` and `day_showcase` for regressions.

## Distant Water from Kinetic (2026-10-25)

Kinetic draws its distant water into the water surface pass (`lod/distant_water_surface.fsh` in Kinetic). With the clear water of this pass its old values showed as a bright turquoise band beyond the render distance: a fixed 6 block thickness let the opaque distant water color shine through as the bottom, and tint alpha 1 now means the highest roughness. Kinetic now writes 64 blocks (deep water, its distant terrain has no sea floor) and alpha 0.545 (roughness about 0.045, the capillary variance lost at that distance), and clips the surface without its dithered tile fade, so the surface has no holes where tiles change. Lowering the distant water to the vanilla water height (8/9 of a block) and writing its side faces were tried and dropped: both exposed distant land skirts and grey streaks at the horizon. A faint line can remain where the 0.11 block step between distant and near water lies. Visual test: `horizon_high_*` and `horizon_*` in a copy of a real world with `helion.visualTest.natural=true`.

Glass, third pass (2026-10-29, owner request: rain on glass like a photo of a wet window): drops come in three bead layers (7, 16 and 40 cells per block, 40 %, 70 % and 85 % of the cells, radius 0.14 to 0.32 of a cell, outline wobbled by 12 % in three lobes) and drops smaller than 1.2 pixels fade out instead of turning into specks. A drop bends the view by 3.5 times its size, so it shows a small upside down image of the world, darkens to 60 % at its rim, brightens its lower rim with focused light and catches a highlight of the reflection. Upright panes have 6 run columns per block (75 % used) whose wavy path wanders by a sine and noise; the trail behind a head is 2.5 blocks long, swells and thins with noise, bends the view across it and has a dark rim and a glint line. Between drops the pane mists over: 50 % of a 10 tap blur of the view behind (radius 0.035 over the distance, at most 3 % of the screen), 15 % grayed and 12 % lighter; drops and trails clear the mist. Visual test: `glass_window_rain`.

Glass, fourth pass (2026-10-29, owner feedback in game: wall windows showed no drops, drops too sparse, runs too fast and cut off): rain on glass ramps in from sky light 0.5 to 0.8 instead of a hard 0.85 cut, so windows in walls under an overhang (whose nearest face lights from inside) get drops too; bead layers are 10, 24 and 56 cells per block (35 %, 75 %, 90 %), rims darken to 70 %, glints only appear on drops of 3 to 8 pixels and more (small ones were white specks); runs slide at 0.06 to 0.2 blocks per second with a 6 block period and a 3 block trail whose last 30 % fades out, so the trail never ends in a cut; mist 60 %.

Glass, fifth pass (2026-10-29, owner feedback in game: wall window drops looked like brown dirt): the highlight of a drop took the reflection, which indoors is the dark wooden room; it now takes the brighter of the reflection and 1.3 times the brightness of the view through the pane, glints show from 5 pixels, and rims darken to 35 % instead of 70 %.

Glass, sixth pass (2026-10-29, owner feedback in game: drops on glass over water looked like flickering specks, the pane looked foggy, glass underground got drops): the mist is gone and so is the smallest bead layer (56 per block, below 3 pixels it shimmered); beads are 8 and 18 cells per block (45 % and 70 %) and trails swell by 30 % instead of 90 %, so they read as continuous lines. Rain no longer follows the sky light of the face (a cave 7 blocks from an opening still had level 8): `WaterRainCover` holds the vanilla rain floor (`MOTION_BLOCKING` height) of 64 x 64 columns around the camera and a glass face within 28 blocks is wet when rain reaches the cell above a horizontal face, or the cell on either side of an upright face, exactly where vanilla rain falls. With water behind the glass, the drops bend the water pixel too (the water is shaded again at the bent pixel when it is still water), so drops over water show a lens view instead of only their rim. Visual test: `glass_lake_rain`, `stained_light_rain`.

Glass, seventh pass (2026-10-29, owner feedback in game: drops on a pane seen at a glancing angle were dark flickering specks, worst along its edge): the drop footprint came from `fwidth` of the plane coordinates, which is undefined in 2 x 2 quads that lose pixels to the early `discard` at glass edges and tiny along one axis at glancing angles. `helionGlassFootprint` now intersects the rays of the neighbor pixels with the glass plane and takes the larger step, so drops fade by their real size on screen (from 1.5 to 4 pixels, glints from 6). Glass, ninth pass (2026-10-29, owner feedback in game: drops still shimmered; the owner asked for a simple wet window, drops, mist and running water, not optical realism): a drop shifted the view by 2.5 times (trails 5 times) its offset from its center, which shows a small upside down image minified 1.5 to 4 times; the sampled texels jumped with every jitter of the temporal anti-aliasing and read as shimmer.
