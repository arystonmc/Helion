# Helion Architecture: Shadows

Part of the [Helion architecture](../ARCHITECTURE.md).

## Shadows

Off by default (`shadows.enabled`). Cascaded shadow maps from the sun, or from the moon while the sun is below the horizon, applied by the shading pass of deferred lighting. `ShadowStage` runs after the terrain pass, so it can read scene depth and the geometry buffer normals, and before deferred lighting, whose shading pass reads its mask. It needs the geometry buffer, deferred lighting and a `SkyLight` from the physical sky, because only the physical sky light separates the sun and moon terms from the sky dome.

| Pass | Writes | Shader |
|---|---|---|
| `shadow_map` | eight persistent D32_FLOAT targets, two sets of one per cascade (`ShadowQuality`: 1024, 1536 or 2048 texels square), only the cascades due this frame | `shadow/caster.vsh`, `caster.fsh`: solid and cutout terrain from the light, depth only, slope and constant depth bias; texels of emissive atlas cells are discarded |
| `shadow_mask` | frame R8_UNORM `helion:shadow_mask` | `shadow/mask.fsh`: per terrain pixel the first cascade that holds it in each set, 4 x 4 tent filtered lookup, the previous set blended in while it fades out, 1 lit and 0 shadowed |

- **Cascades.** `ShadowCascades` splits the view up to the shadow distance (render distance, at most 160 blocks) at 8, 22, 50 and 100 %. Each slice gets a bounding sphere, so its size does not change when the camera turns, and its orthographic projection is snapped to whole texels of the shadow map in world space, so edges do not crawl when the camera moves. The light direction of the shadows turns in steps of 0.1° (`ShadowLight.stepped`), one about every third of a second at normal day speed, and every step fades over from the shadow maps of the previous direction to the new ones (see Update schedule), so shadows move smoothly. A shadow map cannot simply turn with the sun: its texel grid turns with the light, and because the grid is anchored in world space a tiny turn shifts it under the shadows by up to a texel. Steps of 0.25° without a fade made shadows jump about once a second; steps of one tick of sun travel (0.015°) without a fade made every shadow edge slide back and forth by one texel several times a second (measured in a recording of the owner: the edge of a log's shadow moved 4 to 5 pixels at 1080p in a saw tooth every 0.2 s). The depth range extends 256 blocks toward the light, so mountains outside the view still cast into it.
- **Update schedule.** `ShadowCascadeCache` keeps two sets of cascades and decides every frame which cascades of the current set are drawn. When the light takes a step and the current set is complete, the sets swap: the old set stays as the previous one and the new current set is drawn for the new direction (the near cascades at once, the far ones on their turn). Once all four are drawn the mask fades from the previous set to the current one over the time between the last two steps, so the next step arrives when the fade is done. A cascade of the current set is also drawn again when it is older than its refresh time (100 ms for the two near cascades, 200 ms for the third and 400 ms for the far one, so changed blocks reach the shadows within two to eight ticks) or when the camera moved or turned so far that its center shifted by more than a quarter of its radius. The near cascades are drawn at once; the third only on even and the far one only on odd frames, so no frame draws both. The previous set is never redrawn; it is dropped when the camera moved more than a quarter of the near cascade away from where it was drawn. A cascade is drawn at once in any frame when it has never been drawn, the quality or the light (sun or moon) changed, the light turned by more than 0.5° or its radius changed (field of view or render distance). A cascade that waits keeps its shadow map, its matrices and the camera position it was drawn with; the mask moves its matrix by the camera movement since then, and because cascades are snapped to texels in world space the waiting map still shadows exactly the same places. Entity shadows are drawn every frame with the moved matrix of the current set, so they line up with the cached terrain map. Drawing the near cascades every frame cost a frame rate that grew with the frame rate itself; with the schedule a frame at 200 FPS draws a cascade only about every tenth frame.
- **Casters.** `VanillaShadowCasterSource` collects every built chunk section within the render distance that lies inside or toward the light of a cascade drawn this frame, scanning only the sections inside the world space bounds of those cascades, puts them temporarily into `LevelRenderer.visibleSections` and lets vanilla prepare the draws with the light view, once per cascade, then restores the camera's sections. It must use the same draw path as the camera terrain: vanilla keeps the chunk section data of the direct and the multi-draw indirect path in one storage and closes it when the path changes, which would invalidate the camera's draws. Crossed plant planes (grass, ferns, flowers, saplings, detected from the face normal in `caster.fsh`) cast no shadow: they are thinner than a shadow map texel at most distances, so their shadows covered the ground in speckles that shimmered with every camera move. The section list comes from `ShadowCasterSections`, which keeps only sections with solid or cutout geometry and rescans the view area only when the camera enters another section, the view area changes or 20 frames passed; before it, the far cascade prepared about 47,000 mostly empty sections (6.4 ms of CPU time every fourth frame) and the scan itself took 1.8 ms per frame. The caster pipelines are built from the vanilla terrain snippets without any color target: a render pass whose first color attachment is unused crashes in `FrontendCommandEncoder.createRenderPass`. Translucent layers and particles do not cast shadows yet; entities do, see Entities below.
- **Glowing blocks.** A block that emits light casts no sun or moon shadow (owner request, 2026-10-25). `VanillaGlossyCells` marks as emissive (material cell 1) every block atlas sprite that the baked models of light emitting block states use and no state without light uses, on cells without another material (magma stays dry); lava counts as emissive too. The caster pass binds the material mask as `GlossySampler` and discards those texels, and `rtx/sun_shadow.csh` reads the same mask (binding 6) at the committed hit: a first-hit ray that stops on an emitter is traced again for the nearest hit, stepping past emitters up to four times. Sections and entities are traced as two queries, so the emitter test never reads the section surface table for an entity hit. Sprites shared with blocks that do not glow (pumpkin sides of a jack o'lantern, furnace sides) still cast. Without a caster a glowing block in a roof lets the sun through, but the shading scales the sun by the sky light share of the pixel, so under a roof the leak stays faint. Visual test: `emitter_shadow` (stone, glowstone, sea lantern, jack o'lantern and lantern in a row in the morning sun).
- **Ray traced shadow acne (2026-10-26).** The sun shadow rays started 0.02 blocks off the surface reconstructed from scene depth. Depth precision falls with distance, so on flat snow and ground under the low moon the reconstructed point sank below the surface and the rays hit their own block: black stripes across the screen inside the 64 block ray traced range, and a darker ring at its edge. The offset is now 0.02 plus 0.002 per block of distance. Visual test: `night_terrain` and `moonlit_terrain` (15 blocks above the start, midnight looking north and moonrise looking west).
- **Entities.** Entities, block entities and items are dynamic casters, drawn every frame into a separate target per near cascade while the cached terrain maps stay untouched (the static and dynamic split of UE5 virtual shadow maps). Their vanilla pipelines test reversed depth and need a color target, so each entity target has an RGBA8 color and a D32 depth attachment at half the cascade resolution, cleared to 0, and `shadow_entities` replays `PreparedFrame.executeSolid` with the projection swapped for `ShadowCascades.entityProjection`: the cascade's light projection and view, times the inverse of the camera rotation that the entity draws carry as their model view, with depth mirrored. The mask turns the stored depth back with `1 - depth` and keeps the darker of terrain and entity visibility. Only the two near cascades (about 35 blocks) get entity shadows, and only entities the camera sees cast them. Measured on the test platform with an armor stand and a cow: 0.02 ms.
- **Targets.** Each cascade of each set has its own depth target and its own render pass that clears only it, so a cascade that waits keeps its content. The mask binds the current set as `ShadowMapSampler0` to `3` and the previous set as `PreviousShadowMapSampler0` to `3` (the current set again when there is no previous one). The second set doubles the shadow map memory (64 MB more at High). An atlas with one viewport per cascade is not possible: `RenderPass.setViewport` in the Vulkan backend allocates the new viewport with zeroed memory and never sets its depth range, so every depth written through a custom viewport is 0 and the whole world reads as shadowed; and a shared atlas can only be cleared as a whole.
- **Mask.** The mask pass reconstructs the world position from scene depth, offsets it 1.5 texels along the normal and 1 texel toward the light against acne, looks it up in the current set and, while the previous set fades out, in the previous set with its own matrices and light direction, blends the two terrain results by the fade, keeps the darker of that and the entity shadows, and fades to lit over the last 15 % of the shadow distance. Faces turned away from the light stay lit in the mask because the light term is already zero there.
- **Lighting.** Shadows are applied in `deferred_shading`, not in the light term. The shadowed variant (`HELION_SHADOWS`) computes a transmission per color channel: the sun, moon and sky dome light of the pixel's normal with the sun term times `1 - SunShadowStrength × shadow` (moon likewise), divided by the unshadowed sum, then blended toward 1 by how much of the pixel's vanilla light comes from the sky term (so torches and lava fill shadows). The linear shaded color is multiplied by it after the vanilla brightness curve. Applied before that curve, the shadow vanished: the Brightness option lifts every light value with `1 - (1 - x)^4`, which turns 0.3 into 0.97. Lit pixels keep a transmission of exactly 1 and stay identical to the image without shadows; shadowed pixels keep the blue dome light. `ShadowLight` fades the strength to zero between 0.12 and 0.02 elevation of the light, so shadows never pop at sunrise, sunset or moonrise. The visual test platform has a stone pillar and a floating roof away from the light blocks, because block light fills shadows next to emitters.
- **Cost.** Measured on the development GPU at render distance 12 with every effect on (2026-10-16): `shadow_map` 0.73 ms per frame on average with the near cascades drawn every frame and 0.45 ms with the time-based schedule; the benchmark rose from 201.2 / 141.1 / 93.8 to 227.2 / 152.6 / 103.7 FPS (average / 1% low / 0.1% low). Before the first update schedule `shadow_map` took about 2.9 ms at medium quality and render distance 20 on the development GPU (RTX 4050), mostly in the far cascade, which covers about 450 x 450 blocks; `shadow_mask` takes about 0.02 ms. With the schedule a frame draws the two near cascades plus at most one far cascade, and the CPU only prepares the sections of the cascades it draws. In a savanna at render distance 20 with every effect on, the caster preparation fell from about 4.5 ms to under 1 ms per frame on average once empty sections were skipped and the section list was cached, and the frame rate rose from 118 to 181. Culling sections that cannot reach any lit receiver is an open optimization.
