# Helion Architecture: Depth of Field

Part of the [Helion architecture](../ARCHITECTURE.md).

## Depth of Field

Off by default (`image.depthOfField.enabled`, experimental). A gather depth of field in the spirit of Jimenez, "Next Generation Post Processing in Call of Duty: Advanced Warfare" (SIGGRAPH 2014) and the UE5 cinematic depth of field, own implementation, running after motion blur at the output size.

1. **Focus.** `dof_focus` takes the nearer quarter of 25 view depths in a small disc at the screen center (the sky ignored, at most 16384 blocks; when every sample is sky the focus goes to the sky itself, so the sun, the moon and the stars are sharp when you look at them), keeps the stored focus when the target differs by less than 4 % and otherwise moves it in log distance at 4 per second (1 - e^(-4 t)), faster toward a nearer target, in a persistent 1 x 1 target used in turn with its predecessor, so focus follows the crosshair smoothly instead of jumping.
2. **Circle of confusion.** The log of depth over focus, minus a sharp band of 0.25 that widens by 0.08 per log of the focus distance, so looking far keeps the ground around the player sharp. The part beyond the band starts with a quadratic knee over 1.0 and then grows linearly, so the blur starts without a visible edge; it is multiplied by the aperture and limited to 0.35 in front of the focus (negative) and 0.4 behind it. The aperture is 0.6 times the strength and the largest blur 1.5 % of the screen height times the strength, so the default (0.5) gives a gentle lens; a first version with four times the aperture blurred everything off the focus, even the ground in front of the player. Measured cost on the test platform: about 0.07 ms at 854 x 480.
3. **`dof_downsample`** halves the image and stores the circle of the 2 x 2 pixel with the largest blur in alpha. **`dof_gather`** samples 32 golden-angle taps over the circle of the pixel; a tap counts only when its own circle reaches the pixel, so a blurred foreground spreads over sharp things behind it (fading out over two pixels at the edge of its circle), and a tap behind the pixel counts only within the pixel's own circle, so a blurred background never bleeds over a sharp foreground.
4. **`dof_blend`** draws the blurred half resolution image over the sharp scene with an alpha that rises smoothly (smoothstep) from no blur to the largest blur on that side of the focus (at least one pixel), so the focused area stays untouched and the blur fades in over a range of depths. Before, the alpha reached one at 1.5 pixels of blur and the blur started without a knee: looking into the distance with the default strength, the switch from the full resolution scene to the half resolution blurred image happened within about 24 pixel rows of the ground (17 to 26 blocks away) and showed as a line; now it spans about 107 rows (7 to 19 blocks). The sky and the clouds blur like the farthest terrain; keeping them sharp was tried and taken back at the owner's wish.
